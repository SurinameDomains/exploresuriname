Cassavesoep: Surinamese Cassava Soup with Salted Beef

Cassavesoep is comfort in a bowl: a light broth slowly simmered from soup beef and zoutvlees, seasoned with allspice, leaf celery and the aroma of a whole Madame Jeanette pepper. The cassava is cooked in the broth, then diced and placed in each bowl before the hot soup is poured over.

  • Serves 4
  • Prep25 min
  • Cooking1 h 40 min
  • Total2 h 5 min

Ingredients

  • 150 g zoutvlees (salted beef)
  • 200 g soepvlees (beef for soup, such as shank)
  • 1.5 l water
  • 1 onion
  • 6 piment berries (allspice)
  • a few sprigs of selderij (leaf celery)
  • 500 g cassava (about 2 small roots)
  • 1 green Madame Jeanette pepper
  • 1 to 2 stock cubes

Method

  1. Boil the zoutvlees for about 20 minutes in a pan of water to draw out some of the salt. Drain, then cut it and the soup beef into pieces.
  2. Bring 1.5 l of fresh water to the boil. Add both meats, the celery, the onion in pieces and the allspice, and simmer gently for about 1 hour.
  3. Meanwhile peel the cassava: cut the root into sections, slit the skin lengthwise and pull off the brown outer skin together with the pink layer beneath it. Halve the pieces and remove the woody string from the centre.
  4. Add the cassava in large chunks to the soup and cook for 15 to 20 minutes, until a fork slides in easily. Lift it out with a slotted spoon.
  5. Put the whole pepper into the soup and season with the stock cubes. Keep simmering until the meat is completely tender, taking care not to burst the pepper.
  6. Cut the cooked cassava into very small cubes and divide them between soup bowls.
  7. Ladle the hot soup and pieces of meat over the cassava and serve straight away.

Tips

  • Taste before adding all the stock cubes, as zoutvlees can be quite salty.
  • If you prefer, leave the cassava in the pot and serve it in larger pieces with the broth.
  • Abroad, peeled frozen cassava from Surinamese or Latin American shops saves time. Whatever you use, always cook cassava until fully tender.

About this dish

Cassava (kasaba in Sranan) was grown by Suriname's Indigenous peoples long before colonial times and is still a staple on Surinamese tables. This soup belongs to Creole home cooking, where zoutvlees, allspice and a whole pepper flavour many broths. It is an everyday dish, warming and filling.

Before You Go

Health & Vaccinations

What to get before you go and how to stay well once you are here.

The short answer. Suriname has been malaria-free since 2025. Get the yellow fever vaccine, protect yourself against day-biting mosquitoes that carry dengue and chikungunya, and take travel insurance that covers hospital care and repatriation.

Before You Go

Vaccinations

VaccineAdvice
Yellow feverRecommended for everyone from 9 months. A certificate is required if you arrive from a risk country (or spent more than 12 hours in transit there). Get it at least 10 days before travel; certificates are valid for life.
Hepatitis A, typhoid, tetanusRecommended for most travellers, along with routine vaccines.
Hepatitis B, rabiesFor some travellers: longer stays, work with animals or remote trips.
ChikungunyaWorth considering because of the 2026 outbreak; the Dutch RIVM advised it for travellers aged 12 to 64. Your travel clinic can say which vaccine suits you.
DengueThe vaccine is only advised for people who have had dengue before.

Book a travel clinic appointment six to eight weeks before you leave.

Mosquitoes

Dengue, Chikungunya and Zika

The Aedes mosquitoes that spread dengue, chikungunya and Zika bite during the day, in the city as well as the countryside. Use a repellent with DEET or icaridin, wear light long sleeves at dusk, and sleep under a net or with air conditioning or screens.

Chikungunya returned in January 2026 and spread to most districts; health officials said in August that the epidemic phase was over, with low-level circulation continuing. Zika is a risk in pregnancy: pregnant women should discuss the trip with their doctor.

Malaria is no longer a risk: the WHO certified Suriname malaria-free on 30 June 2025.

Food and Water

Water, Food and Swimming

  • Treated tap water in Paramaribo is generally safe to drink. In the districts and the interior, stick to bottled or filtered water.
  • Street food and roti shops are part of the experience; choose busy places where food is cooked fresh.
  • Schistosomiasis is present at very low levels. Organised swims in fast-flowing creeks and rivers are common, but avoid wading in still fresh water such as ditches and ponds.

Sun and Heat

Sun, Heat and Humidity

Suriname lies just north of the equator: the UV index often reaches 11 or more around midday, and daytime temperatures sit around 29 to 32 °C all year. Use sunscreen, wear a hat, drink plenty of water and plan walks for the morning. Today’s UV and rain forecast are on our weather page.

If You Get Ill

Hospitals, Pharmacies and Insurance

The main hospitals with an emergency department in Paramaribo are the Academisch Ziekenhuis Paramaribo (AZP), the Diakonessenhuis and the St. Vincentius Hospital. Emergency rooms are busy: for problems that are not life-threatening, use a GP out-of-hours post (huisartsenpost) instead.

Pharmacies (apotheken) are easy to find in Paramaribo. Today’s on-call pharmacies are on our Daily Notices page.

Hospitals usually ask for payment upfront, and serious cases are sometimes flown abroad. Take travel insurance that covers hospital care and repatriation. In an emergency call 115 (police) or 113 (ambulance).

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there malaria in Suriname?

No. The World Health Organization certified Suriname malaria-free on 30 June 2025, the first country in the Amazon region to reach that status. Malaria tablets are no longer needed.

Do I need a yellow fever vaccination for Suriname?

It is recommended for all travellers aged 9 months and over. A certificate is required if you arrive from a country with yellow fever risk, including a transit of more than 12 hours there.

Is there chikungunya in Suriname?

Yes. An outbreak began in January 2026 and passed its epidemic peak by August, but the virus still circulates. Use repellent day and night; ask a travel clinic about the chikungunya vaccine.

Is the tap water safe to drink in Suriname?

Treated tap water in Paramaribo is generally considered safe. Outside the capital and in the interior, drink bottled or filtered water.

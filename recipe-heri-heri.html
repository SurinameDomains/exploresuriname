Heri heri: Boiled Tubers and Plantain with Salt Cod

In Sranan: heri heri

Heri heri is honest, filling food: a platter of boiled cassava, sweet potato and plantains, each with its own texture and sweetness, served next to a pan of salt cod stewed with tomato, onion and hot pepper. It is simple to make and carries a lot of meaning in Suriname.

  • Serves 4
  • Prep30 min
  • Cooking45 min
  • Total1 h 15 min

Ingredients

Bakkeljauw

  • 500 g bakkeljauw (salted cod)
  • 8 tablespoons oil (about 120 ml)
  • 1 large onion, finely chopped
  • 3 cloves garlic, finely chopped
  • 1 fresh hot pepper (green, or a Madame Jeanette), finely chopped
  • 2 large tomatoes, diced
  • 2 sprigs leaf celery, chopped

Tubers and plantains

  • 1 green plantain
  • 1 ripe plantain
  • 500 g cassava (about 2 roots) or napi (a small native yam)
  • 2 sweet potatoes (about 500 g)
  • salt
  • 2-4 hard-boiled eggs (optional)

Method

  1. Rinse the bakkeljauw and boil it in fresh water until the flesh flakes apart easily, about 15-20 minutes. Very salty fish can first be soaked in cold water for a few hours.
  2. Drain, remove skin and bones, rinse the flakes briefly under the tap and squeeze them dry. Leave a little salt in: it seasons the whole dish.
  3. Heat the oil and gently fry the onion, garlic and hot pepper until soft. Add the fish and fry for a few minutes, then add the tomatoes and celery.
  4. Cover and stew over low heat for about 30 minutes, stirring now and then, until the tomato has melted into a rich, oily sauce.
  5. Meanwhile peel the plantains, cassava and sweet potatoes and cut them into large chunks. Remove the woody core from the cassava.
  6. Boil the green plantain in salted water for about 30 minutes. In another pot of salted water, cook the cassava and sweet potato for 20-25 minutes, adding the ripe plantain for the last 15 minutes.
  7. Keep the green plantain in its hot cooking water until serving, so it does not turn tough. Drain the rest.
  8. Arrange the tubers and plantains on a platter with the stewed bakkeljauw alongside, and halved boiled eggs if you like.

Tips

  • Zuurgoed (Surinamese pickles) such as pickled cucumber, onions or birambi on the side cut through the richness.
  • Some cooks season the bakkeljauw with a teaspoon of curry powder, a spoonful of tomato paste and a bay leaf.
  • Abroad, frozen cassava and plantains from tropical shops work well; boneless bakkeljauw saves time.

About this dish

Heri heri belongs to the Creole kitchen and is widely seen as a dish that recalls the era of slavery, when boiled roots, plantains and salted fish were everyday food. Today it is eaten with pride, especially around Keti Koti on 1 July, when Suriname commemorates the abolition of slavery. Heri means 'whole' in Sranan.

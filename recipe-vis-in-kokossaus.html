Vis in kokossaus: Fish in Turmeric Coconut Sauce

This fragrant fish stew is quick enough for a weeknight yet pretty enough for guests. Thick pieces of fish poach in a golden coconut sauce scented with ginger, turmeric and salam leaves, while slices of pan-fried pumpkin add sweetness. Finish with fresh leek and serve with white or yellow rice.

  • Serves 6
  • Prep20 min
  • Cooking25 min
  • Total45 min

Ingredients

  • 1 kg firm white fish fillet
  • 400 ml coconut milk (1 tin)
  • 250 g pumpkin
  • 1 piece fresh ginger, about the length and thickness of a finger
  • 1 tbsp kunir (ground turmeric)
  • about 5 salam leaves (Indonesian bay leaves, sold dried in Asian and Surinamese shops)
  • ½ tsp salt or 1 stock cube (10 g)
  • 75 ml oil
  • 100 ml water
  • 1 leek or a few spring onions

Method

  1. Cut the fish into large chunks, rinse them and leave to drain in a colander.
  2. Peel the ginger and slice it thinly. Peel and seed the pumpkin and cut it into thin slices.
  3. Heat the oil in a wide, heavy pan with a lid and fry the pumpkin slices on both sides until they just start to colour. Lift them out onto a plate.
  4. Fry the ginger briefly in the same oil, then stir in the kunir for a few seconds.
  5. Pour in the coconut milk and water, add the salam leaves and the salt or stock cube, and bring to the boil.
  6. Lay the fish in the sauce in a single layer and spoon some sauce over it. Lower the heat, cover and simmer for 10 minutes.
  7. Tuck the pumpkin slices in between the fish and cook, covered, for another 5 minutes.
  8. Meanwhile wash the leek and cut it into thin rings, including some of the green part. Scatter it over the fish just before you turn off the heat.
  9. Lift the fish and pumpkin onto a serving dish and pour the sauce over.

Tips

  • Shake the pan gently instead of stirring, so the pieces of fish stay whole.
  • For extra heat, let a whole Madame Jeanette pepper simmer in the sauce and take it out before serving.
  • Yellow rice cooked with coconut milk and kunir makes a festive partner for this dish.

About this dish

Vis in kokossaus is a home-style dish rather than a fixed classic. It relies on seasonings that Javanese-Surinamese cooking made part of kitchens across the country: salam leaves, kunir, ginger and coconut milk. Fish is central to eating in Suriname, and a mild coconut sauce like this suits many kinds of local fish.

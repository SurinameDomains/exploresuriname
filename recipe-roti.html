Roti: Surinamese Split-Pea Flatbread with Masala Chicken

Soft roti with a thin layer of spiced split peas inside, served with a pot of masala chicken and potatoes: for many Surinamese, nothing beats it. It takes some patience, mostly in the kneading and rolling, but the steps are simple and the result makes a whole table happy.

  • Serves 6
  • Prep1 h 30 min
  • Cooking1 h 10 min
  • Total2 h 40 min

Ingredients

Roti dough

  • 500 g self-raising flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • about 250 ml water
  • 60 g margarine and 2 tablespoons oil, for brushing

Split-pea filling

  • 125 g yellow split peas
  • 2 teaspoons cumin seeds (djira)
  • 2 cloves garlic
  • 1 small onion
  • 1 small green chilli, seeds removed
  • ½ teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons oil

Masala chicken

  • 1 kg chicken, cut into pieces
  • a splash of vinegar, for rinsing
  • 6 tablespoons oil
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • 3 cloves garlic, crushed
  • 1 Madame Jeanette pepper (fragrant, very hot Surinamese pepper), chopped
  • 3 tablespoons Surinamese masala (a curry spice blend)
  • 1 tomato, chopped
  • about 250 ml water
  • 500 g potatoes, peeled and cut into chunks
  • 1 sprig leaf celery
  • salt and black pepper

Method

  1. Make the dough: sift the flour and baking powder into a large bowl and work in the water a little at a time. Knead for at least 15 minutes, until soft, smooth and easy to roll, then cover and leave to rest for at least an hour.
  2. Meanwhile rinse the split peas and boil them for 25 to 30 minutes, until just tender but not mushy. Drain very well and grind them to fine, dry crumbs (in a food processor, or crush them with a rolling pin).
  3. Toast the cumin in a dry pan until dark and fragrant. Pound it with the garlic, onion and green chilli into a paste, then mix this paste and the salt through the ground peas.
  4. Heat the oil in a frying pan and cook the pea mixture, stirring all the time, until it is dry and crumbly. Let it cool completely.
  5. Divide the dough into 6 balls. Flatten one into a small disc, put a heaped spoonful of filling in the centre, then pull the edges up over it and twist them to seal.
  6. Set the parcel seam-side down on a floured surface and roll it out carefully into a thin round of about 25 cm. Work gently so the dough does not tear and let the filling escape.
  7. Heat a tawa (flat roti griddle) or a heavy frying pan until very hot; a pinch of flour should brown at once. Melt the margarine with the oil.
  8. Cook each roti for about a minute, brush the top with the margarine mixture, flip it and brush the other side. Cook until both sides show light brown spots, then stack the roti in a bowl under a clean cloth to keep them soft.
  9. For the masala chicken, rinse the chicken pieces with water and a splash of vinegar and let them drain. Heat the oil, fry the onion, garlic and pepper until soft, then stir in the masala for one minute.
  10. Add the chicken and turn it until coated in masala, then add the tomato, water, potatoes, celery, salt and black pepper. Cover and simmer for 30 to 40 minutes, until the chicken is tender and the potatoes are soft.

Tips

  • Potato filling: replace the split peas with about 250 g potatoes, boiled until just cooked, mashed very fine and seasoned the same way.
  • Serve with stewed kousenband (long yard beans) and a little sambal on the side.
  • No time for roti? Masala chicken is also delicious with plain white rice.

About this dish

Roti came to Suriname with Hindostani contract workers from India and has become a dish all Surinamese love. The split-pea version is the classic. Roti is eaten with the hands: tear off a piece and use it to scoop up chicken, potato and kousenband. Roti shops are found all over Paramaribo and in Surinamese neighbourhoods in the Netherlands.
